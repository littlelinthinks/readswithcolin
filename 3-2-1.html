3-2-1: On reading slowly, what a good book sounds like, and one question about your attention 3-2-1：慢读书、一本好书的声音，与一个关于你注意力的问题

3IDEAS FROM COLINCOLIN 的 3 条思考

I.

Read at the pace of comprehension, not the pace of pages. Most readers measure progress by how many pages they've turned. But the right pace is the one where every sentence still has time to land. When you feel yourself skipping ahead, that's not speed, that's a quiet alarm — your mind is asking for more time. 以理解的节奏读，而不是翻页的节奏。 大多数读者用翻过的页数衡量进度。但真正合适的节奏，是每个句子都有时间落地的节奏。当你发现自己跳着读，那不是快，那是心里在响警报 —— 你的脑子需要更多时间。

II.

A great book is one you can argue with. The books that change you most are rarely the ones you agree with. They're the ones that make you defensive, that interrupt your assumptions, that leave a margin-note you've been thinking about for days. Agreement is comfortable; argument is generative. 一本好书，是一本你可以与之争论的书。 改变你最多的书，几乎从不是你认同的书。它们是那些让你戒备、中断你的假设、让你留下批注并琢磨几天的书。认同很舒服；争论才有生成性。

III.

Reading is the cheapest form of mentorship. A $20 book can give you 30 years of someone's hard-won thinking. A two-hour flight can be the entire semester of a course you didn't take. Compared to therapy, coaching, or a degree, books are the most leveraged hour of learning available — and they fit in your back pocket. 阅读是最便宜的导师。 20 美元的一本书能给你一个人 30 年磨出来的思考。一段两小时的飞行，可以是一门你没上过的课的整个学期。比起咨询、教练、学位，书是杠杆率最高的学习时段 —— 而且装得进你的后口袋。
